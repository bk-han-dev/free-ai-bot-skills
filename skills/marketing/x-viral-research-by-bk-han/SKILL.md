# X Viral Research By BK Han

Turn this week's real X trends and the account's own results into one draft post that grows followers and builds the brand. Draft only: never publish. A human approves every post before it goes out.

## Out of scope
Out of scope: This skill covers X only. For research across several platforms, use Viral Research To Draft By BK Han (viral-research-to-draft-by-bk-han). This skill doesn't publish or schedule posts: it stops at a draft for human approval.

## Inputs
- Brand brief: who the brand is, its audience, voice, frameworks, and motto.
- The X account handle, plus any rivals or similar brands worth comparing.
- Optional topic or keyword brief, such as an SEO/AEO weekly brief.
- The brand's site URL for a soft call to action, and any event link that should come first.

## Steps
1. **Review the account's own posts.** If X data tools are available, pull the account's recent posts with real impressions and views. Break them down by format (single tweet, thread, video, image), by theme, and by month, and sort them by impressions. Write two short lists: what to post more of and what to post less of. If the tools can't return impressions, say so and skip this step. Never guess numbers.
2. **Find content gaps.** Compare what X has been talking about in the brand's niche over the last 7 days with how much YouTube and other coverage exists on the same topics. Topics with lots of X talk but little coverage are openings. Pick 1 to 3.
3. **Check brand sentiment now and then.** Monthly, or when something big happens, look at how X talks about the brand compared with one or two similar brands: overall tone, which topics win or lose, and the top posts. Use only posts you actually saw.
4. **Research what is spreading now.** Search the web for X posts, threads, and format write-ups from the past 7 to 10 days, and open the best 2 or 3 sources. Look at hooks, thread shapes, reply bait, and quote formats.
5. **Name the pattern.** In 2 to 4 lines, say what the pattern is, why it works, and which source you saw it in. If you couldn't open live X, say so. Never make up view counts, quotes, handles, or posts you didn't see.
6. **Pick one angle.** Combine the viral pattern with a content gap from step 2 and the post-more-of list from step 1, in the brand's own lane. Fit this week's topic or keyword brief if there is one.
7. **Draft one post.** Write one tweet or a short thread of up to 4 tweets, starting with a hook and an outline. Copy the structure, never anyone's wording, and keep the brand's voice.
8. **Check the length.** Count characters with a script and keep every tweet under 280.
9. **Add calls to action.** Use one soft main call to action, such as a reply keyword like CREW or AGENT, and at most one extra. Never write "Comment YES". Keep links out of the first tweet: put the site in the last tweet or a self-reply, tied to the post (for example, "full DINO breakdown on <site>"). For event posts, the event link comes first.
10. **Image note.** Add one line describing a simple card if it would help, otherwise write "text-only".
11. **Do a manual-review pass and deliver.** Before handing it over, check that the idea is actually good and on-brand. Return the account review, the content gap, the pattern, the full draft with character counts, the image note, and source URLs. Mark it as draft only.

## Output format
ACCOUNT REVIEW (post more of / post less of, or "no data"), CONTENT GAP, VIRAL PATTERN (2 to 4 lines plus URLs), DRAFT (numbered tweets with character counts), IMAGE NOTE, SOURCES.

## Sample test prompts
1. "Research what's going viral on X this week and draft one thread for an AI coach whose audience is SEA solopreneurs, with the soft reply call to action CREW."
2. "Review my last month of X posts by format and theme, tell me what to post more and less of, and draft one tweet from the best gap. Draft only, with the site link in a self-reply."
3. "Find a topic X is buzzing about this week that YouTube hasn't covered much in the AI agents niche, and turn it into one draft thread."
