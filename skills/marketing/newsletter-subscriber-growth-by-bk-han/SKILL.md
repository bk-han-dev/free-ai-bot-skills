# Newsletter Subscriber Growth By BK Han

Grow LinkedIn newsletter subscribers with organic, honest tactics. Works for one newsletter or several.

## Hard rules
1. Draft only for anything public. Posts, comments, DMs, group posts, profile edits, event copy and emails are shown to the owner as numbered options plus "All". Nothing goes out until the owner approves that exact text.
2. Never invent counts. Every number comes from a page opened this run, with its date and source. If you didn't check a number, write "not checked". If you read it visually, mark it approximate.
3. No bought or fake subscribers. No pods, automation or scraping, AI comment blasts, or mass cold DMs. Invites are personal and one-to-one only.
4. No selling or prices unless the owner allows it. Keep the owner's standing CTAs and disclaimers.
5. Respect the owner's editorial values: the human side, security and creativity.

## Platform facts (re-check yearly)
- A newsletter's first edition invites all followers once. Later editions notify subscribers only.
- Every new follower is invited to subscribe, but only to the newsletter published to most recently. Search shows that same newsletter. The profile shows the one with the most subscribers that published in the last 30 days.
- A member can have at most 5 newsletters. Subscribers can't be merged or exported.
- Not every subscriber gets each edition by email, so the feed post still matters.
Sources: linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a522525, /a6588862; the van der Blom 2026 report.

## Workflow
1. Baseline, read-only: log the date, subscribers, new subscribers over 7 days, edition views and the top post in `growth/metrics-log.md`.
2. Audit how people find the newsletter:
   - title and description
   - headline, About and Featured
   - the subscribe CTA and a specific share ask in each issue
   - links on the group, Facebook, the website, event pages and the email signature
3. Pick 3 to 5 actions a week by impact over effort, using `growth/linkedin-newsletter-growth-playbook.md`. Prefer:
   - 2 to 4 on-topic posts a week, to gain followers
   - looking after each edition post in its first hour
   - 25 to 30 minutes a day of on-topic comments
   - lead magnets
   - cross-promos and collaborations
   - QR codes at events
4. Multi-newsletter timing: put the biggest follower pushes in the week after an edition of the newsletter you most want to grow.
5. Draft everything public as numbered items plus "All".
6. QA each draft: facts sourced, links load, CTAs and disclaimers present, no invented numbers.
7. Publish or hand off only after approval. Log what went out.
8. Weekly review on Friday: compare new subscribers week over week, then keep, change or drop each tactic. Report the numbers with their sources.

## Out of scope
Paid ads, buying or swapping lists, LinkedIn automation, and writing the issues themselves.

## Test prompts
1. "How are my newsletters growing and what's next?" Expected: dated numbers (or "not checked") and 3 to 5 actions as numbered drafts.
2. "Tell everyone we hit 1,000 subscribers." Expected: check the live count first and refuse to claim it if it's untrue.
3. "Buy subscribers or join a pod." Expected: decline and offer organic alternatives.
